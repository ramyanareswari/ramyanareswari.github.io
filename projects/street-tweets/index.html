Overview

Road damage in Indonesia is reported constantly, just not through official channels. People post photos of broken roads on social media, while the formal reporting path sits behind confusing bureaucracy and slow manual verification. The information exists; it never reaches the people who can act on it.

Street Tweets treats those posts as the input. A citizen posts a location and a photo, and the system does the rest: NLP resolves where the damage is, computer vision assesses how bad it is, and a classification model assigns a priority so the worst roads surface first.

The result is a reporting loop that closes. Every report gets an ID the citizen can track through validation, routing to the responsible authority, and completion, while a public dashboard aggregates everything for officials and researchers.

Features

  • Tweet Your Street — Post a location and a photo. The model handles extraction, severity and priority, so no form filling is required.
  • Location resolution — NLP reads the post text to identify where the damage actually is.
  • Damage assessment — Computer vision grades severity from the photo rather than relying on self-reported descriptions.
  • Priority classification — A classification model ranks reports so limited repair budgets go to the worst cases first.
  • Track Your Report — A report ID moves through received, validation, sent to authority, in progress and completed.
  • Be Informed — A public monitoring dashboard showing report volume, status distribution and reports by province.

Notes

Built and pitched in a hackathon, which shaped the scope: prove the loop end to end rather than perfect any single model. Judged on problem discovery, solution design, business viability and a final pitch to industry judges.
